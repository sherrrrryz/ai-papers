# From Control to Foresight: Simulation as a New Paradigm for Human-Agent Collaboration

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ1 Planning · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
A perspective paper for the CHI 2026 Workshop on Human-Agent Collaboration. It argues that today's agent oversight is "pointwise and reactive": users approve or correct one step at a time and must mentally simulate consequences. It proposes "simulation-in-the-loop", where the agent externalises several possible future trajectories, each annotated with risks, trade-offs and uncertainties, so the user can explore before committing. There is no empirical study; it illustrates the idea with a hypothetical trip-planning scenario.

## Key findings
- Conceptual claim: control over steps is not enough; users need foresight of downstream effects, since errors compound in long tasks.
- The framework separates simulation (sensemaking over alternatives) from planning (finding one optimal path).
- Design space has three axes: lookahead depth (foresight vs overload), exploration breadth (comparison vs cognitive load) and granularity (rich but slow vs fast but possibly hallucinated).
- Open challenges: reliability of LLM self-simulation, choosing which futures to show, and cognitive load of comparing trajectories.

## Why it relates to your idea
Your sample-size slider ("how much lift do you expect?" answered in users and days) and your risk cards are small simulations: they let a non-technical person see the consequence of a choice before the experiment runs. The paper also applies to your AI assistant, whose write actions (branch, PR, rewrite) currently stop at confirmation cards, which is exactly the pointwise control the authors critique.

## What you can take from it
- Use it as a framing citation for RQ1: show consequences, not parameters. The slider should show what each choice leads to ("detect a 2% lift: about N users, D days") rather than asking for an effect size.
- Consider making confirmation cards show the outcome of an action, for example the events that will appear in the event panel after the instrumentation PR, not only the action itself.
- Their "granularity" axis supports your choice to compute durations with the standard formula in code rather than letting the model estimate them: fine-grained, reliable simulation over a fast LLM guess.
- Keep alternatives few and meaningfully different (they warn about breadth overload): for example, two or three duration options rather than a continuous range.

## Limits for your use
- Workshop perspective paper with no user evaluation; cite it for framing only, not evidence.
- Its domain is autonomous multi-step agents, not experiment planning by non-technical users.
