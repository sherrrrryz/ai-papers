# Fostering Appropriate Reliance on Large Language Models: The Role of Explanations, Sources, and Inconsistencies

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · Verifying AI analysis · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Two studies. A think-aloud study with 16 people using ChatGPT-4o on objective questions identified features of responses that shape reliance. Then a pre-registered, within-subjects 2x2x2 experiment with 308 US Prolific participants varied answer correctness, presence of an explanation, and presence of clickable sources from a hypothetical LLM ("Theta", 50% accurate by design). Naturally occurring inconsistencies in explanations were analysed as an extra factor.

## Key findings
- Explanations raised agreement with correct answers (67.2% to 78.2%) but also with wrong ones (78.2% to 82.8%), so they did not help people catch errors.
- Sources alone cut overreliance on wrong answers (78.2% to 68.2%) and still raised agreement with correct ones (73.4%).
- Inconsistent explanations reduced overreliance on wrong answers: 69.7% agreement vs 83.3% for consistent ones (p = .002).
- Explanations or sources raised confidence (from 4.55 to up to 5.83 on 1-7), even on incorrect answers.
- Only 25.8% of source opportunities were clicked; 189 of 308 participants never clicked a source.

## Why it relates to your idea
Your page has the model write explanations: the inferred hypothesis (RQ1), risk cards, and the results captions (RQ3). This paper predicts that fluent explanations will raise trust in those outputs whether or not they are right, which is exactly the "automation breeding blind trust" risk you name. It also suggests what helps: linking claims to checkable evidence.

## What you can take from it
- Present the hypothesis card as a claim with evidence slots (the specific diff lines and the events that support it), not as a polished paragraph. Sources reduced overreliance; explanations did not.
- Keep the evidence one glance away rather than behind a click. Most participants never clicked sources, so in your page the evidence should sit on the prototype itself (highlighted change, event firing).
- Your planted trap in Phase 2 is the right design: the paper's key result only appears when the AI is sometimes wrong. Measure agreement with the AI caption separately for trap vs non-trap items, as they measured agreement on correct vs incorrect answers.
- Add a confidence rating to your comprehension test; the paper shows confidence rising with explanations independently of correctness.
- Cite it when you say model-written captions must be labelled AI-generated and kept short.

## Limits for your use
- Objective trivia questions with a single response, not interpreting experiments or plans.
- Hypothetical LLM at 50% accuracy, chosen to be hard; your AI will be right more often, which may change the effects.
- General Prolific population, not designers or PMs.
