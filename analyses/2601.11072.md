# More Human or More AI? Visualizing Human-AI Collaboration Disclosures in Journalistic News Production

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ2 Communicating · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Co-design sessions with 10 designers and HCI experts produced 69 disclosure concepts, from which four prototypes were built: a Textual Disclosure label, a Role-based Timeline (person and robot icons), a Task-based Timeline (five workflow stages with hover details) and a Chatbot with preset questions. A within-subjects lab study with 32 participants crossed these with two article versions (primarily human vs primarily AI written), measuring perceived collaboration, clarity, informativeness, time and eye tracking, plus interviews.

## Key findings
- Text labels were least effective: weakest on showing specific steps and most often ranked last, described as legalistic.
- Both timelines were rated clearest and gave the best overview (p = .003 for clarity; p < .001 for overview); the Task-based Timeline was strongest on specific steps and most often ranked first, though 9 participants missed its hover details.
- The Chatbot was most informative and in-depth but hardest to understand, took longest (M = 150 s vs about 110 to 116 s) and drew the most gaze (partial eta squared .62 for gaze duration).
- Format changed perception: the Role-based Timeline inflated perceived AI contribution on human-written articles, and the Task-based Timeline shifted AI-written articles toward human involvement.
- 8 participants said disclosures made them read more critically.

## Why it relates to your idea
Your page mixes AI-generated content (hypothesis, risk cards, captions) with code-computed numbers and human edits. This paper shows that how you disclose who did what changes what readers believe. It bears on RQ2 (a visual brief vs a document) and on your "labelled AI-generated" captions in the results view.

## What you can take from it
- Do not rely on a plain "AI-generated" text label; mark provenance per element (computed by code, written by AI, edited by you), tied to the specific part of the page, similar to the task-based view.
- Avoid hover-only disclosure; 9 of 32 missed it.
- Expect a chat interface to cost time and clarity; your on-page assistant should not be the main way to learn what the page means.
- Support for RQ2: structured visual formats outperformed text on overview and clarity, consistent with your claim that a visual brief communicates better than a document.
- Borrow the measure "perceived share of AI vs human contribution" to check whether participants can tell which parts of the brief the AI inferred.

## Limits for your use
- News readers judging articles, not teams reading experiment briefs.
- Chatbot condition confounded with extra detail; short-term, low-stakes stimuli.
