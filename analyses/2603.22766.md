# From Overload to Convergence: Supporting Multi-Issue Human-AI Negotiation with Bayesian Visualization

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ3 Reading results · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
A within-subjects experiment (CHI 2026) with N = 32 participants who played a tenant negotiating rent terms against a GPT-4 landlord. The number of issues varied (1, 3, 5 or 7), and participants negotiated with and without a decision-support visualization. A Bayesian model updated from the AI's offers fed two widgets: a Negotiation Horizon Grid (one row per issue, green bands marking options acceptable to both sides) and a Global Convergence Panel (a bar that narrows as the agreement space shrinks). The baseline always came first.

## Key findings
- Without support, payoff held steady through 3 issues and then dropped sharply at 5 (d = 1.12) and 7 (d = 0.97). The dimensionality effect was large (η² = 0.478).
- With the visualization, payoff did not change significantly across 1 to 7 issues. The interface effect was large (ηp² = 0.659).
- Joint payoff was also higher with support (ηp² = 0.300), so the user's gain did not come at the AI's expense.
- People made fewer, smaller and more focused moves, with less backtracking. NASA-TLX mental demand, effort and frustration were lower, especially at high dimensionality. SUS was 81.0.
- The authors recommend keeping the tool "prosthetic rather than prescriptive": highlight where acceptable options lie, but do not recommend a specific offer.

## Why it relates to your idea
RQ3 asks how to show uncertainty, segment sample sizes and guardrails without readers giving up. This paper shows that an uncertainty visualization designed for non-experts can stop performance collapsing as the number of variables grows. Your results view has the same problem: one primary metric is easy, but a primary metric plus guardrails plus segments is several issues at once.

## What you can take from it
- Pair a per-item view with an overall progress cue, as they did. For your results view this could be one row per metric or segment (with a band showing a plausible range and a mark when the sample is too small) plus a single "how sure are we overall" summary that leads the page.
- Follow "prosthetic, not prescriptive": show where the evidence is strong or thin and let the person decide whether to ship, which matches your conclusion-first view without the AI giving the verdict.
- Consider showing extra structure only once complexity passes a threshold, for example revealing segment detail only when there are more than two segments or a guardrail moves.
- Borrow their measures: NASA-TLX alongside correctness, so you can show that misreading fell without readers giving up.

## Limits for your use
- A negotiation game, not reading experiment results, with a controlled task and a convenience sample of 32.
- Fixed condition order (baseline first) means learning effects cannot be ruled out, and the paper has some reporting inconsistencies in its tables.
