# The Impact of Revealing Large Language Model Stochasticity on Trust, Reliability, and Anthropomorphization

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ3 Reading results · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
A within-subjects study with 19 university participants (mostly PhD students, skewed toward computer science), presented at the TREW workshop at CHI 2024. Each person saw GPT-4 responses (temperature 1) to one question type (factual, logic riddle, factual with justification, or advice) under three conditions: one response, ten responses, and ten responses with a cognitive support tool (Positional Diction Clustering, which colour-highlights shared structure across responses). Measures were NASA-TLX, a human-computer trust scale and Godspeed anthropomorphism items, with think-aloud and interviews.

## Key findings
- No significant effect of condition on workload, trust or anthropomorphism in the omnibus tests; a few exploratory, uncorrected differences between question types.
- Qualitatively, seeing many responses let participants cross-check, and consistency was read as reliability.
- Inconsistent answers to logic questions reduced trust; for advice questions, variation was welcomed as breadth.
- 6 of 19 said a single response was not enough to judge reliability.
- The authors caution that consistency is not accuracy: a wrong answer may appear most often.

## Why it relates to your idea
RQ3 asks how to show uncertainty so misreading drops without readers giving up. This paper tests one way of revealing uncertainty (show the spread) with a support aid to manage load. The null quantitative result, plus mixed qualitative reactions, is a useful warning that showing variability is not automatically helpful.

## What you can take from it
- For model-written captions in the results view, consider showing that the caption is one of several possible readings, or regenerating to check stability, but do not treat agreement across runs as correctness.
- If you show uncertainty as multiple outcomes (for example several plausible lifts), pair it with an aid that highlights what is common, as their clustering did, so load does not rise.
- Use NASA-TLX (or its short form) in your study to check the "without giving up" half of RQ3; this paper is an example of measuring load alongside trust.
- Reactions depended on the kind of question; separate factual readouts (did the metric go up?) from judgement calls (should we ship?) when deciding how much variation to show.

## Limits for your use
- Workshop paper, small N (4 to 5 per question type), no significant main effects.
- Shows LLM text variability, not statistical uncertainty in experiment results.
- Technically trained participants, unlike your designers and PMs.
