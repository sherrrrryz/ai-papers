# From Accuracy to Readiness: Metrics and Benchmarks for Human-AI Decision-Making

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · Verifying AI analysis (evaluation design) · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Lee's CHI 2026 Extended Abstract is a position and framework paper with no empirical study. It argues that human-AI evaluation should ask "how ready is the human-AI team?" rather than "how good is the model?", proposes a four-part metric taxonomy computed from interaction traces, and links it to an Understand-Control-Improve (U-C-I) lifecycle, illustrated with a hypothetical clinical triage case.

## Key findings
- Outcome metrics: team gain over human-only and AI-only baselines, avoidable error vs an oracle, error recovery vs amplification.
- Reliance metrics: accept-on-wrong, changed-to-wrong, override frequency and timing, reliance slope.
- Safety metrics: AI-harm (AI turns a correct human decision wrong), near-misses, rollback and escalation events.
- Learning metrics: calibration gap (confidence vs correctness), transfer, stability under shift.
- U-C-I: Understand (failure examples), Control (calibration cards, regions-of-no-use, safe levers with preview, rollback and audit trail), Improve.

## Why it relates to your idea
Your evaluation explicitly measures comprehension, not accuracy, and your risk section worries about blind trust in automation. This paper gives you named reliance and safety metrics to frame what you measure in the planted-trap task and around the AI assistant's confirmation cards.

## What you can take from it
- Record three things per decision: the participant's initial view, what the page or AI suggested, and the final decision. That lets you report accept-on-wrong (shipping despite the trap) and changed-to-wrong (switching after reading an AI caption).
- Ask a confidence rating with each restatement and decision to report a simple calibration gap, which is cheap with 5-8 people.
- Count "escalation" as a desirable outcome: a participant who says "I'd ask an analyst" is a safety signal, matching your goal of knowing when to call an analyst.
- Cite the Control stage (preview, rollback, audit) to justify your confirmation cards and branch/PR-never-merge design.

## Limits for your use
- Extended abstract with no validation; the author calls it a starting point.
- Metrics like team gain assume ground-truth labels and many trials; with a small N, report them descriptively.
