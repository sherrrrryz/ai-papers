# CritiqueCrew: Orchestrating Multi-Perspective Conversational Design Critique

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ2 Communicating · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Chen et al. built CritiqueCrew, a Figma plugin where three GPT-4o roles (UX, Product Vision, Engineering) critique a design, a coordinator merges them into a prioritized agenda that surfaces conflicts, and selecting an issue zooms to the component with a previewable, one-click, undoable fix. A formative study interviewed 15 professionals (designers, PMs, front-end engineers). Two within-subjects studies with 24 professional designers each compared it with a static checker and with a single "unified expert" version.

## Key findings
- Formative themes: designers need multi-faceted feedback to resolve cross-functional conflicts, solutions in context rather than problem lists, and partnerships that keep decisions with them.
- Vs static checker: issue coverage 9.58 vs 7.96 (p = .006), expert-rated solution effectiveness 5.22 vs 4.30 (p < .001, d = 1.19), SUS 77.29 vs 55.52, NASA-TLX 40.57 vs 51.99, trust 3.76 vs 2.89.
- Separate roles vs unified expert: coverage 11.08 vs 9.92 and effectiveness 5.77 vs 5.02 (both p < .001), with expert raters at ICC about 0.8-0.85.
- Authors argue role orchestration, not one model, drives trust and creativity support.

## Why it relates to your idea
It works in the designer's own tool and bridges UX, PM and engineering views, the same cross-functional gap your brief targets. For RQ2, it shows designers respond better to feedback anchored on the artifact (zoom to component) than to a separate list, which matches your claim that experiment information belongs inside the operable prototype.

## What you can take from it
- Anchor every risk card and flagged segment to a place on the prototype, the way CritiqueCrew zooms to a component; a list of risks detached from the screens is the "problem auditor" pattern they beat.
- Consider framing risk cards by role ("Analyst: sample too small for new users"; "Engineer: checkout step not instrumented"), since role separation raised coverage and effectiveness.
- Borrow their blind expert rating with ICC for your analyst's review of AI-generated hypotheses and risk cards; even one rater can use a fixed 7-point rubric.
- Cite the formative themes as evidence that designers want decision authority retained, supporting confirmation cards.

## Limits for your use
- Measures design quality, not comprehension of a shared artifact by a team.
- Baseline was one commercial plugin; parts of the discussion and limitations were outside what I could read.
