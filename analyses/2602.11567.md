# Behavioral Indicators of Overreliance During Interaction with Conversational Language Models

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · Verifying AI analysis (evaluation design) · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Liu et al. had 77 university participants work with a GPT-4 chat in a split screen on three tasks (survival-item ranking, summarizing a 700-word article, planning a Copenhagen trip), each about 15 minutes. The LLM was made to inject plausible misinformation (a wrong ranking; 11 subtle factual errors in the article task; 20 verifiable errors in the trip task), and overreliance was measured as how much of it survived in the participant's output. Browser logs were encoded into action vectors, embedded and clustered to find behaviors linked to overreliance.

## Key findings
- Of 154 clusters, 54 were valid and 18 were salient for high or low overreliance, giving five patterns.
- High copy-paste: high-overreliance users made about 5-6 copy/paste actions per 60 s with little editing; low-overreliance users made 1-3 and edited afterward.
- Low-overreliance users spent time comprehending the task page before opening the LLM; this never appeared in high-overreliance clusters.
- High-overreliance users switched to the LLM for nearly every decision (3-4 switches in 10 s), navigated coarsely, and sometimes hesitated but still accepted the wrong answer.
- Suggested mitigations: auto-verification for heavy copy-paste, task roadmaps and confirmation for weak comprehension, chunking, staged review with friction, and cognitive forcing functions.

## Why it relates to your idea
It uses the same method as your Phase 2 "planted trap": inject a plausible error, then score whether people catch and correct it. It also gives behavioral signals to watch while participants use the brief page and the AI assistant, beyond the final ship/no-ship answer.

## What you can take from it
- Operationalize the trap like they did: define in advance what counts as "caught" (mentioned, corrected, or decision changed) and score it per participant.
- Log simple behaviors in your probe (time on the prototype and hypothesis card before opening the assistant, number of assistant calls, whether confirmation cards are accepted unedited) and report them next to the trap outcome.
- Borrow "comprehend before consulting": make the old/new comparison the first thing on the page, before the assistant is available.
- Use friction at the confirmation card (e.g., require one edit or one checked box before "apply") for the pattern of accepting despite hesitation.

## Limits for your use
- Students on everyday tasks, not practitioners reading experiments; misinformation was simulated.
- The clustering pipeline needs far more than your 5-8 participants; take the patterns and measures, not the method.
