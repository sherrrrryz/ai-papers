# Same Performance, Hidden Bias: Evaluating Hypothesis- and Recommendation-Driven AI

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · Verifying AI analysis · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Benk and Miller ran a web experiment with 290 UK Prolific participants (from 320 recruited) on word-association puzzles: on each trial, decide whether a theme is present. A simulated GPT-4 aid at fixed 70% accuracy was shown in five between-subjects conditions: control, recommendation-driven (RD, a yes/no verdict), RD plus explanation, RD plus hypothesis exploration, and hypothesis-driven (HD, no verdict; participants explore evidence and alternatives). They used Signal Detection Theory to separate sensitivity (d') from the evidence threshold (criterion c).

## Key findings
- Accuracy did not differ between conditions (Brier scores, p > .05), and sensitivity showed no condition effect.
- Every recommendation condition lowered the evidence threshold relative to control (RD beta = -0.19, p = .002; with explanation -0.34; with hypotheses -0.21), while HD did not differ from control (beta = -0.09, p = .137).
- RD and RD-with-explanation thresholds kept dropping from training to testing; HD and control did not.
- HD was slowest (M = 48.5 s per trial) and RD fastest (M = 28.9 s).
- Task knowledge predicted sensitivity but not the threshold, which the authors read as experts being no less susceptible.

## Why it relates to your idea
This speaks directly to RQ1 and the hypothesis card. Your AI infers a hypothesis from the code diff; if it is shown as a verdict ("this change will raise checkout conversion"), users may accept it on less evidence without any visible drop in accuracy. It also matters for the Phase 2 results view, where "conclusion first" is a recommendation.

## What you can take from it
- Show the inferred hypothesis as a claim with evidence slots (which diff lines, which events support it) rather than a recommendation, because only the hypothesis-driven design kept evidence standards at the unassisted level.
- Adding an explanation to a verdict did not fix the bias (it was the largest shift), so a rationale under the hypothesis card is not enough on its own.
- For the results view, consider pairing "conclusion first" with the evidence the reader must check, and test whether your planted trap catches a lowered threshold.
- Borrow the measurement idea: score not only whether people restate correctly, but whether they accept a wrong AI claim, i.e. track false acceptances separately from misses.
- Expect the hypothesis-style version to take longer; report time alongside comprehension.

## Limits for your use
- Workshop paper; word puzzles with lay crowdworkers, not product decisions.
- Twenty trials with a fixed-accuracy simulated AI; the expert comparison is thinly described.
