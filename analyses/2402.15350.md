# Farsight: Fostering Responsible AI Awareness During AI Application Prototyping

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ1 Planning · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Farsight is an in-situ tool for people prototyping LLM apps with prompts. It shows an alert based on similar AI incidents, a sidebar of related news and LLM-generated use cases, and a "Harm Envisioner": an editable tree from prompt summary to use cases, stakeholders and harms, where users can edit, delete, regenerate, classify and rate nodes. The authors ran a co-design study with 10 AI prototypers at Google and a user study with 42 AI prototypers across six conditions, comparing Farsight, Farsight Lite (alert plus sidebar only) and a baseline harm-modelling guide.

## Key findings
- After using Farsight or Farsight Lite, participants independently identified 2.42 and 3.00 more harms in a new task (p = .0028 and .0003; d = 1.21 and 1.27); the baseline guide group did not improve (-0.14).
- With the tool in hand, Farsight conditions produced about four more harms than the guide (d = 1.57 and 1.48), but likelihood and severity of harms did not improve.
- Both tools were rated easier to use than the baseline; the tree visualisation was praised and the incident news panel scored lowest.
- Participants valued being able to edit or delete AI output (sense of agency), and shifted from failure modes toward end-users, indirect stakeholders and cascading harms. Some LLM output was generic or far-fetched.

## Why it relates to your idea
This is the closest precedent for your risk cards (RQ1): an LLM-generated, editable pre-mortem placed inside the prototyping workflow rather than in a separate document. It shows that such scaffolding transfers to independent thinking afterwards, which supports your claim that the page should help people "ask the right question", not just hand them answers.

## What you can take from it
- Make risk cards editable and dismissable, with a "regenerate" option; participants valued agency over AI-generated harms.
- Place risks in situ and use progressive disclosure, so they do not interrupt; the co-design found an anthropomorphised assistant distracting, a caution for your on-page AI assistant.
- Borrow the pre/post transfer design: ask participants to list risks for a new change before and after using your page, then count risks found unaided.
- Note that quantity rose but quality (likelihood, severity) did not; have your analyst blind-review risk cards for plausibility, as you plan, and expect low agreement (their raters' kappa was 0.14 and 0.09).
- Cite it for positioning: existing AI experiment tools lack a pre-mortem; Farsight shows one works in a neighbouring domain.

## Limits for your use
- AI harms, not experiment validity risks such as novelty effects or conflicting tests.
- Participants were Google AI prototypers, more technical than your designers and PMs.
- Small cells (7 per condition).
