# Where Are We So Far? Understanding Data Storytelling Tools from the Perspective of Human-AI Collaboration

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ3 Reading results · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Li, Wang and Qu surveyed 60 papers on human-AI collaborative data storytelling tools from VIS and HCI venues up to June 2023. Two authors coded each tool by workflow stage (analysis, planning, implementation, communication) and by the primary role of human and AI in each stage (creator, assistant, optimizer, reviewer).

## Key findings
- AI is concentrated in implementation (45 of 58 tools); planning had AI in 14 of 29 tools, and communication in only 2 tools, both as assistants.
- The two dominant patterns were AI-creator with human-optimizer (32 times) and human-creator with AI-assistant (20 times).
- Reviewer roles, both AI and human, were rarely explored.
- Lessons: automatic data-fact recommendation can oversimplify insights and miss domain knowledge; AI planning tends to follow data relationships rather than context or audience.
- Opportunities include using LLMs for text and for reviewing stories from audience perspectives, and studying collaboration among multiple humans (analysts, editors, presenters) and AI.

## Why it relates to your idea
Your Phase 2 results view is a data story: conclusion first, funnel overlaid on the prototype, 2 to 3 next hypotheses, with the model writing only captions. This survey gives you a vocabulary to position that design: charts from templates (no AI creator), model as caption assistant, and the non-technical reader as reviewer who decides.

## What you can take from it
- Describe your division of labour with their framework: in analysis, code is creator; in implementation, templates are creator and the model is an assistant for captions; in communication, the human is the reviewer. This is a sharp positioning sentence for the proposal.
- Cite the gap: reviewer roles and the communication stage are rarely supported, while your project targets exactly a non-expert reviewing and acting on AI-assisted results.
- Use the warning that data-fact recommendation oversimplifies to justify labelling captions as AI-generated and keeping the ship decision with people.
- Their call to study multiple humans with AI fits your RQ2 framing of a team (designer, PM, analyst) sharing one brief.

## Limits for your use
- A literature survey with no user data, and its corpus ends in mid-2023, before most LLM-based tools.
- Storytelling tools are mostly for authors of stories, while your users are readers of results.
