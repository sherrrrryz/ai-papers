# Investigating the Effects of LLM Use on Critical Thinking Under Time Constraints: Access Timing and Time Availability

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · Verifying AI analysis · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Zhi, Kumar and Lee ran a 4 x 2 between-subjects experiment with 393 US Prolific participants (cells of 39 to 52). Acting as city council members, participants read seven documents of varying relevance and trustworthiness and wrote a reasoned decision on a water-contamination cleanup proposal. A GPT-4o assistant restricted to the documents was available early (first third), continuously, late (final third), or not at all, under 10 minutes (insufficient) or 30 minutes (sufficient).

## Key findings
- Under 10 minutes, early (3.80) and continuous (3.49) LLM access beat no LLM (2.75) and late (1.86) on essay score.
- Under 30 minutes the pattern reversed: late (5.77) and no LLM (5.76) trended above continuous (4.71) and early (4.51).
- With more time, the late group gained 3.91 essay points and the no-LLM group 3.01, while early access gained almost nothing; unique arguments rose by +1.80 (late) and +2.10 (none) vs. +0.28 (early).
- With sufficient time, continuous access lowered document recall (2.03 vs. 2.99) and trustworthiness-rating accuracy (34.0% vs. 44.3%); late access reduced myside bias (3.42 vs. 4.41).
- Self-assessed critical thinking barely varied, so self-reports missed these effects.

## Why it relates to your idea
Your evaluation is a short comprehension test, and your page has an AI assistant available throughout. This paper shows that when the AI is available and how much time people have can flip whether it helps or hurts reasoning. That bears on both your study procedure and on when the assistant should appear on the page.

## What you can take from it
- Fix and report the time budget for your comprehension test; a very short window favours whichever condition hands people the answer early, which could inflate your page's advantage over the document brief.
- Consider making the assistant available only after the participant has stated the hypothesis and primary metric in their own words, following the authors' "independent attempt first" recommendation.
- Score restatements and trap detection as performance; do not rely on self-rated understanding, which this study found insensitive.
- Use the "early support for orientation, not solutions" idea: under time pressure, the assistant can point to where to look (event panel, risk card) rather than giving the ship/no-ship answer.

## Limits for your use
- Crowdworkers on a civic document task, not designers reading an experiment brief.
- Single session with two fixed durations; essay grading used an LLM grader validated on 100 participants.
