# Interview-Informed Generative Agents for Product Discovery: A Validation Study

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ1 Planning · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Wang and Siu (Adobe Research) interviewed 51 US knowledge workers for 90 minutes each, then had them rate four AI document-workflow concepts (6-item TAM, Net Promoter Score, open-ended feedback) twice, three days apart, to measure self-consistency. They built a GPT-4o agent per person from the interview transcript and compared it with demographics-only and no-information agents across 3,060 responses.

## Key findings
- At the individual level, human test-retest agreement was 0.446; the interview-based agent reached 0.300 (about 67% of that), barely ahead of demographics-only (0.259) and no-information (0.256), with no significant difference among agents.
- At the population level, the interview-based agent came closest to the human distribution (Wasserstein 0.227 Likert, 0.487 NPS, vs. 0.175 / 0.211 for the humans' second attempt); the no-information agent collapsed to mostly 6 and 8.
- Agents reproduced the main themes but not the edges: about 30% of humans outright rejected the Audio Assistant concept, while only one agent did; agent answers were uniformly constructive.
- An agent run cost about $1.27 and 4 minutes vs. about $12.50 and 30 minutes for a human.
- The authors call agents "distribution-calibrated but identity-imprecise", suitable for early screening, not for individual insight.

## Why it relates to your idea
Your RQ1 system has an AI infer user intent (the hypothesis) from a code change. This paper is a measured caution on how far AI inferences about users can be trusted in product work: plausible on average, systematically too positive, and wrong on the specific case. That supports keeping the hypothesis editable and the risk cards pessimistic.

## What you can take from it
- Use it to justify why the hypothesis card must invite the designer's own intent: AI inference captures the generic story but misses the specific person or context.
- The positivity bias (agents rarely rejected concepts) is an argument for risk cards as a deliberate counterweight to an optimistic AI-generated hypothesis.
- For your positioning, cite it as evidence that AI in PM workflows is moving into concept testing, and that your project addresses a different stage (instrumentation and reading results).
- Their test-retest design is a reminder to separate noise from error; if you have your analyst re-review a subset, you get a rough reliability check.

## Limits for your use
- Concept testing of new products, not inferring hypotheses from code; agents simulate users, not planners.
- One domain, four concepts, N = 51, and LLM judges with moderate agreement (kappa 0.53).
