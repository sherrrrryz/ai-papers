# AI, Help Me Think—but for Myself: Assisting People in Complex Decision-Making by Providing Different Kinds of Cognitive Support

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · Verifying AI analysis · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
An exploratory within-subjects study (CHI 2025) with N = 21 participants, 18 of them with ETF investing experience. People revised a simulated ETF portfolio using two GPT-4o assistants. RecommendAI returned ETFs to add or remove. ExtendAI asked the user to write a rationale first, then embedded bold feedback inside that rationale pointing to blind spots, without naming specific ETFs. Data were logs, questionnaires and interviews, analysed descriptively with no significance tests.

## Key findings
- 45% of RecommendAI-based decisions came straight from the AI's suggestions. With ExtendAI, 23% of final trades differed from the user's own initial plan, so the user's plan stayed central.
- ExtendAI took longer (17.5 vs 8.6 minutes, 8.4 of them writing the rationale) and had a higher NASA-TLX (57.0 vs 52.5).
- Confidence was higher with RecommendAI (86% vs 67%), but satisfaction after seeing outcomes was higher with ExtendAI (67% vs 43%).
- People browsed fewer options with RecommendAI (12.9 ETFs viewed vs 19.7 with ExtendAI).
- Preferences split almost evenly (11 vs 10). Participants felt they sometimes followed RecommendAI "without much thought," while ExtendAI gave more agency.

## Why it relates to your idea
This is the clearest empirical version of your "AI proposes, people decide" principle. It bears on your AI assistant and on the RQ1 hypothesis card. If the AI hands over a finished hypothesis and metric, many people will adopt it as-is. If it responds to the person's own stated intent, they stay engaged, at a cost in time and effort.

## What you can take from it
- Consider an ExtendAI-style step in RQ1: ask the designer for one line on "what did you hope this change would do?" before revealing the AI-inferred hypothesis, then show where they differ. This turns the hypothesis card into a comparison, not a recommendation.
- Report confidence and correctness separately in your evaluation. The higher confidence with recommendations here is exactly the blind-trust pattern your planted trap is designed to catch.
- Use the adoption measure: count how many hypothesis or risk-card fields participants accept unchanged versus edit. This is cheap to log.
- Budget for the time cost. ExtendAI roughly doubled assistant time, so keep your intent prompt to one line.

## Limits for your use
- Investing with synthetic data, not product experiments, and participants were mostly experienced investors, not designers.
- Small N, descriptive statistics only, and partly recruited from personal networks, which mirrors your own constraints.
