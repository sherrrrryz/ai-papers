# From Text to Trust: Empowering AI-assisted Decision Making with Adaptive LLM-powered Analysis

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ3 Reading results · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Li et al. (CHI 2025) studied whether GPT-4 analyses of each feature's contribution help people use an AI recommendation on income prediction (AI 76% accurate) and recidivism prediction (62%). Study 1 randomized Prolific workers (134 and 150 after checks) to no analysis, analyses one at a time, or all at once. They then trained a model of how each analysis shifts decisions and built an algorithm that picks which analysis to show next, evaluated in Study 2 with 447 and 397 participants against several baselines.

## Key findings
- Study 1: showing LLM analyses, sequentially or all at once, did not significantly change accuracy, overreliance or underreliance; the authors cite overload and random ordering.
- Study 2: the adaptive algorithm gave the highest accuracy on both tasks and significantly reduced overreliance vs control and other conditions (e.g., recidivism vs control p = .002).
- It needed fewer rounds (income 2.99 vs 4.88 sequential; recidivism 2.50 vs 4.71), and still won when rounds were matched, so selection mattered, not just quantity.
- Underreliance was mostly not reduced.
- The algorithm chose supporting analyses when the AI was right and contradicting ones when it was wrong; the authors flag manipulation risk and that LLM analyses are not the model's real reasoning.

## Why it relates to your idea
Your results view gives the model a caption-only role. This paper shows that simply adding LLM-written explanations does not improve reliance; what helps is choosing few, decision-relevant ones. That supports RQ3's concern with what to show so non-statisticians do not misread, and informs how many captions and next hypotheses to show.

## What you can take from it
- Keep captions few and decision-relevant: lead with the conclusion, then at most one or two analyses chosen for this result (e.g., the guardrail that moved, the segment too small to trust), not one per metric.
- Include a caption that argues against shipping when the data warrants it; contradicting analyses were what reduced overreliance when the AI was wrong.
- Cite Study 1's null result to justify not relying on AI explanation alone and keeping numbers in templates.
- Borrow overreliance and underreliance as outcomes for the trap: following a misleading result vs dismissing a correct one.

## Limits for your use
- Crowd workers on tabular prediction, not practitioners reading A/B results.
- The adaptive algorithm needs hundreds of participants to train; take the "few, selected, including counter-evidence" lesson, not the method.
