# Who's the Leader? Analyzing Novice Workflows in LLM-Assisted Debugging of Machine Learning Code

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · Verifying AI analysis · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Bo et al. ran a formative, in-person think-aloud study (Tools for Thought workshop paper) with 8 university students familiar with, but not expert in, machine learning. Each had 60 minutes and ChatGPT 4o-mini to debug a Random Forest script on the UCI Adult Income data with three planted errors (overfitting, unshuffled split, class imbalance), plus a pre-task ML knowledge quiz and post-task survey and interview.

## Key findings
- Holdout F1 ranged from 0 to 0.28 (best achievable about 0.32, unmodified code 0.16); four participants fixed two errors and two fixed all three.
- Performance correlated strongly with the ML knowledge quiz (r = .93, p < .001) but not with self-reported experience.
- Two interaction modes: "leading" the LLM with planned questions vs being "led-by" it with open questions. Four of the five top scorers led in at least half their turns; led-by users showed high trust and more over-reliance, while leaders tended toward under-reliance.
- Metacognitive pitfalls: wrong premises in questions got irrelevant advice, broad questions returned 10+ options, and small parameter differences were hard to verify.
- Participants felt they learned, but confidence did not rise and learning was mostly syntactic.

## Why it relates to your idea
It supports your motivation that domain knowledge, not tool access, predicts whether someone can judge AI output in a low-verifiability task. For your AI assistant, it warns that a non-technical person who lets the assistant lead will drift into over-reliance, which your confirmation cards and planted-trap evaluation are meant to catch.

## What you can take from it
- Design the assistant so the person leads: offer a few framed questions ("Is the primary metric the one your change affects?") instead of open chat, since leaders did better.
- Return a short prioritized list (2-3 options), not 10+; the paper names option overload as a failure.
- Have the assistant correct wrong premises proactively (the authors flag ChatGPT's sycophancy); e.g., if a PM asks for a metric the change cannot move, say so on the card.
- For your evaluation, code each participant's assistant turns as leading vs led-by and relate that to whether they caught the trap; also add a brief knowledge probe rather than relying on self-reported experience.

## Limits for your use
- N = 8 CS students, one model, one task; workshop status.
- Over- and under-reliance were described qualitatively, not counted.
