# Design Principles for Generative AI Applications

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ2 Communicating (positioning your design principles) · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Weisz et al. (IBM) developed six principles and 24 strategies for generative AI UX over four iterations: a literature review; feedback from a workshop of about 50 researchers and an internal guide seen by over 1,000 practitioners; a modified heuristic evaluation where 18 design practitioners assessed 9 commercial apps and found 286 examples; and workshops with two product teams that produced 46 and 56 design ideas.

## Key findings
- Six principles: Design Responsibly, Design for Mental Models, Design for Appropriate Trust and Reliance, Design for Generative Variability, Design for Co-Creation, Design for Imperfection.
- Trust strategies include calibrating with explanations, giving rationales, using friction against overreliance, and signifying the AI's role.
- Imperfection strategies include making uncertainty visible, evaluating with domain-specific metrics, and offering ways to improve outputs.
- Every strategy had at least one real-world example; rated clarity rose from 4.29 to 4.58 of 5 after revision.
- The authors present them as principles applied with judgment, not rules; Trust and Reliance was rated less relevant for creative tools.

## Why it relates to your idea
One of your outputs is a short set of design principles. This paper is the obvious general-purpose reference to position them against, and several of its strategies map onto your features (friction, signifying AI role, visible uncertainty).

## What you can take from it
- Frame your principles as domain-specific instances of theirs: "every AI write stops at a confirmation a non-technical person can read" instantiates "use friction to avoid overreliance"; labelling captions as AI-generated instantiates "signify the role of the AI".
- Your "results belong on the interface, not a separate dashboard" has no direct counterpart in their list, which lets you claim it as new for experimentation.
- Use "evaluate outputs using domain-specific metrics" to justify computing sample size and lift in code rather than letting the model do arithmetic.
- Borrow their validation idea at small scale: check your principles against 2-3 existing tools (GrowthBook, Statsig, Amplitude) and note where each meets or misses them.

## Limits for your use
- Mostly one organization; no user-outcome evaluation of the principles.
- General purpose, so it does not speak to experiments, statistics or non-technical readers specifically.
