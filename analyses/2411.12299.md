# Could Humans Outshine AI in Visual Data Analysis?

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ3 Reading results · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
A crowdsourced experiment presented at the TREW workshop at CHI 2024. 74 US Prolific workers each did 24 trials: give a prior belief about a bivariate relationship on an everyday topic, view a scatterplot sample, then give a posterior estimate of slope and uncertainty using sliders (1,776 responses). Sample size (7, 15 or 30 points) and social consensus were manipulated; sample extremeness varied naturally. Humans were compared with an informed Bayesian agent (using the participant's own prior) and a flat-prior Bayesian agent.

## Key findings
- With typical samples, humans were less accurate than both agents (-1.62 vs informed Bayesian, -2.37 vs flat-prior).
- With extreme samples, humans roughly matched the informed Bayesian and beat the flat-prior agent; their advantage grew with extremeness.
- Humans were less swayed by extreme samples (sensitivity 13.53) than the informed Bayesian (18.61) and flat-prior agent (31.73).
- Participant sensitivity rose with sample size (8.57 small, 12.90 medium, 19.21 large) and was higher under low consensus (17.46 vs 9.65).
- Overall the statistical models were more accurate, since extreme samples are rare.

## Why it relates to your idea
Your results view must help non-statisticians read uncertainty and small segments (RQ3). This paper shows that lay readers bring priors and gut reactions to charts, and those heuristics sometimes protect them against misleading small samples, though on average they are less accurate than a model. It bears on your segment flags and your planted trap.

## What you can take from it
- Supports your division of labour: let code compute the estimate and flag small segments, but do not suppress the reader's intuition; ask "does this match what you expected?" before showing the result.
- Prior elicitation is a usable move: on the sample-size slider you already ask for expected lift; show it next to the observed result in Phase 2 so readers see the gap.
- Design your trap around an extreme small segment (for example a big lift in a tiny segment); this paper predicts lay readers may discount it, which tests whether your flag adds anything beyond intuition.
- Borrow the prior/posterior slider procedure as a comprehension measure of how much participants update.

## Limits for your use
- Workshop paper with preliminary analysis; crowd workers, not designers or PMs.
- Scatterplot slope estimation, not reading A/B test results, conversion funnels or confidence intervals.
- Sample extremeness was not controlled.
