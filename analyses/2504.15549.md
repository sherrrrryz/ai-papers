# Do It For Me vs. Do It With Me: Investigating User Perceptions of Different Paradigms of Automation in Copilots for Feature-Rich Software

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ2 Communicating · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Khurana et al. built two copilots: AutoCopilot, which fully automates a task from a prompt, and GuidedCopilot, which automates only trivial user-initiated steps, gives step-by-step visual guidance, and lets users edit extracted entities before automation runs. 20 participants (9 CS, 11 non-CS; none had used Figma) did Google Sheets and Figma tasks with each in a within-subjects study, followed by a Wizard-of-Oz design exploration in Photoshop with 10 participants.

## Key findings
- Task completion: 35.0% vs 88.5% in Sheets and 20.0% vs 55.0% in Figma for Auto vs Guided (both p < 0.0001); accuracy 12% vs 82% and 5% vs 40%.
- 18/20 (Sheets) and 16/20 (Figma) felt more in control with GuidedCopilot; 16/20 said it helped them learn the steps.
- AutoCopilot needed 192 attempts vs 89, and about 75% of AutoCopilot attempts were undoing wrong automation; users called it a "gamble".
- In the follow-up, in-context preview clips helped all 10 build a mental model; experts wanted to skip steps, novices valued milestones and validation.
- Time savings for AutoCopilot were perceived, not measured.

## Why it relates to your idea
Your AI assistant can rewrite the brief, open a branch and submit a PR. This paper is direct evidence that "do it with me" beats "do it for me" for control and correctness among non-experts, including in Figma, which supports your confirmation-card design and the "AI proposes, people decide" principle.

## What you can take from it
- Let the user edit the assistant's extracted entities before it acts (their GuidedCopilot did this): e.g., the confirmation card for "add instrumentation" lists the event names and the screens they attach to, editable before the PR.
- Show a preview of the effect in context before applying, like their preview clips: highlight on the prototype where a new event will fire.
- Avoid one-shot full automation for the brief; the undo burden (75% of attempts) is an argument you can cite for staged writes.
- Use their three factors (familiarity, task type, intent) to justify more guidance for designers new to experimentation and an option to skip for experienced PMs.

## Limits for your use
- Tasks were software operation, not interpreting or communicating an experiment.
- N = 20 (and 10 Wizard-of-Oz); it tests automation level rather than prototype vs document, so it supports RQ2 only indirectly.
