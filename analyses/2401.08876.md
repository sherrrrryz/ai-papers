# Evaluating the Utility of Conformal Prediction Sets for AI-Advised Image Labeling

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ3 Reading results · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Zhang, Chatzimparmpas, Kamali and Hullman ran a pre-registered online experiment with 599 Prolific participants (150 per condition) who labeled 16 ImageNet images each, 4 in-distribution and 12 corrupted out-of-distribution (OOD). AI advice from a classifier with 82.5% top-1 accuracy was shown four ways: no advice, Top-1 with softmax, Top-10 with softmax, or a conformal prediction set (RAPS, 95% coverage). They analysed accuracy and semantic distance of errors with Bayesian mixed-effects models, and measured willingness to pay for each display.

## Key findings
- Any AI display raised accuracy from about 41% (no advice) to roughly 63 to 66% overall.
- On easy OOD images Top-1 (89.6%) beat prediction sets (79.5%); on hard OOD images with small sets, prediction sets helped (45.1% vs. 36.3% Top-10 and 16.9% Top-1).
- Set size mattered: on hard in-distribution images, small sets gave 74.2% accuracy vs. 52.8% for large sets.
- Willingness to pay was similar across displays ($1.70 to $1.84), and the display that helped most on hard cases was not valued more.
- The authors note users' understanding of the coverage guarantee was not studied.

## Why it relates to your idea
RQ3 asks how to show uncertainty so readers without statistics misread less without giving up. This is a careful, pre-registered test showing that an uncertainty display helps on hard cases and can hurt on easy ones, and that people do not value the display that helps them. That maps onto how your results view should show effect ranges and flagged small segments.

## What you can take from it
- Keep uncertainty compact: like small prediction sets, a narrow, plainly labelled range ("likely between +1% and +4%") beats a wide set of possibilities that adds load.
- Make uncertainty conditional on difficulty: show a simple point conclusion when the result is clear, and switch to the range view (and the small-sample flag) when the result is ambiguous or a segment is underpowered.
- Include both an easy and a hard (trap) case in your Phase 2 test, because a display can look worse on clear results and better on hard ones; one case alone could mislead you.
- Do not use preference as your outcome: participants' willingness to pay did not track benefit, so measure correct ship decisions and misreadings directly.

## Limits for your use
- Image labeling by crowdworkers, not A/B results read by designers; uncertainty is over class labels, not effect sizes.
- One model, one dataset and five corruption types.
