# User Experience Design Professionals' Perceptions of Generative Artificial Intelligence

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ2 Communicating · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Semi-structured, roughly 60-minute online interviews (CHI 2024) with N = 20 UX designers, conducted April to July 2023. Eleven had over 5 years of experience and seven under 3; ten were in Europe and ten in the US, mostly at large companies. Participants watched four demo videos of GenAI tools as probes rather than using them. Five researchers ran a thematic analysis that produced 415 statement cards and six themes.

## Key findings
- All 20 participants described GenAI as assistive. Repetitive tasks were seen as replaceable, but empathy, stakeholder communication and creative judgment were seen as human work, and participants wanted to keep final decision-making power.
- Tool friction was a major pain point, including hard design-to-development handoffs and fragmented tools. Stakeholder communication took much of designers' time.
- Many doubted GenAI's accuracy and practicality because reviewing its output still takes considerable effort.
- Senior designers worried that juniors would skip learning fundamentals.
- Participants hoped for more visual and accessible AI tools and clear metrics for judging AI output. The authors recommend contestable, transparent AI that humans can review and override.

## Why it relates to your idea
This is background on your target population. It shows that designers want AI that assists while they decide, that they already spend much of their time communicating with stakeholders, and that handoff to engineering is a known pain point. Those are the gaps your experiment brief addresses, both in communicating a change to the team (RQ2) and in keeping the person in charge of the AI assistant.

## What you can take from it
- Use it to support your claim that designers will accept an AI that proposes but not one that decides. The "keep final decision-making power" theme matches your "AI proposes, people decide" principle and your confirmation cards.
- Cite the handoff and stakeholder-communication findings to motivate RQ2: the brief is a communication artifact for a group that already spends much of its time explaining work to others.
- The wish for "more visual" AI tools and "clear metrics for evaluating AI output" supports showing experiment information on the prototype rather than in a text document.
- Note the gap: these designers discussed AI for ideation, writing and simple code, not for experimentation or data. Nobody in this study talks about reading results, which is where your project adds something new.

## Limits for your use
- Perceptions from watching demos, collected in 2023, not behavior with a working tool.
- Says nothing about statistical literacy or experiment planning; use it for population framing only.
