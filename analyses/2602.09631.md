# A Multiliteracy Model for Interactive Visualization Literacy: Definitions, Literacies, and Steps for Future Research

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ3 Reading results · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Molina León, Bach, Valentim and Elmqvist propose a model of interactive visualization literacy, "the ability to explore data through interactive visualizations." It crosses Norman's three gulfs (goal formation, execution, evaluation) with three levels (strategic, tactical, operational) into nine literacies: four established (interaction, device, visualization, visual) and five new (analysis, task, tool, workflow, insight). They coded 15 systems across six scenarios and ran an observational think-aloud study with 9 participants on two systems (SDG Atlas and EgoLines).

## Key findings
- Only 3 of 9 participants discovered without help that Atlas pages could be scrolled to reveal more content; 2 never noticed the search bar.
- All participants had good device literacy, but they struggled most with goal formation and analysis, and with understanding EgoLines' visual encoding.
- The authors found no observed situation the model could not explain; strategic literacies such as workflow literacy were hardest to observe.
- Existing visualization literacy tests cover only one of the nine literacies.
- Recommendations: make interactivity discoverable, give in-situ help, offer alternative interactions for different literacy levels, and avoid blaming users for design problems.

## Why it relates to your idea
Your results view and live event panel are interactive visualizations aimed at readers without statistics. This model gives you vocabulary for why a reader might misread: not only chart reading (visualization literacy), but not knowing what question to ask (goal formation), not finding the toggle (interaction literacy), or not turning a pattern into a ship decision (insight literacy).

## What you can take from it
- Use the nine literacies as a coding scheme for where participants went wrong in your Phase 2 test, so "where people were misled" is reported in a structured way rather than ad hoc.
- Make interaction discoverable: if the funnel overlay or segment drill-down is hidden behind hover or scroll, expect many readers never to find it, as 6 of 9 missed scrolling.
- Lead the results view with the goal (the conclusion and the question it answers) to support goal formation, the gulf participants struggled with most.
- Cite it to argue that measuring comprehension of an interactive brief needs more than a standard visualization literacy test such as VLAT.

## Limits for your use
- A theoretical model with a small exploratory study (N = 9), not tested with product practitioners.
- Strategic literacies, the most relevant to ship decisions, were the hardest to observe.
