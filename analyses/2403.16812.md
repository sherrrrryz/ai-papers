# Towards Human-AI Deliberation: Design and Evaluation of LLM-Empowered Deliberative AI for AI-Assisted Decision-Making

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · Verifying AI analysis · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
The authors propose Human-AI Deliberation: human and AI state opinions per dimension (attribute), align to find disagreements, discuss them, and update. Their Deliberative AI uses GPT-3.5-turbo only as a conversational bridge to a domain model (a linear model with SHAP values), so facts come from the model and data, not the LLM. A between-subjects study on a graduate admissions task compared Deliberative AI, a conventional XAI assistant and a human-alone condition (153 valid Prolific participants with admissions experience: 48, 51, 54), on four hard cases where the AI was about 50% accurate.

## Key findings
- Accuracy was higher with Deliberative AI than XAI (0.598 vs 0.524, p < .05), but not significantly above human alone.
- Overreliance dropped (0.47 vs 0.65, p < .001); underreliance did not change (0.32 vs 0.29).
- The cost was time and comfort: median 236 s per decision vs 33 s for XAI; trust was lower (4.47 vs 5.52) and satisfaction lower than human alone.
- 43 of 48 said discussion helped them decide; 21 said it helped them spot AI problems. Others found AI updates too large (11) or unexplained (13), and five felt reduced agency.

## Why it relates to your idea
Your RQ1 asks how a non-technical person can judge an AI inference and add their own intent. This paper shows that breaking an AI recommendation into dimensions and asking for the human's view on each reduces blanket acceptance. That maps onto your hypothesis card, primary metric and guardrails: each is a separate dimension the person can agree or disagree with, rather than one brief to accept whole.

## What you can take from it
- Split the AI's plan into separately confirmable parts (hypothesis, primary metric, guardrails, duration, each risk card), each with an agree/edit/disagree control, instead of one "accept brief" button.
- Copy the architecture: the LLM explains, but numbers come from code. This matches your "arithmetic to code, explanation to the model" division and gives you a citation for it.
- Their reliance measures (overreliance, underreliance, switch fraction) can structure your Phase 2 analysis of whether people follow the AI's ship recommendation on the trap dataset.
- Budget for the trade-off: deeper engagement cost about 7x the time and lowered satisfaction, which speaks directly to your RQ3 concern about readers giving up.

## Limits for your use

- AI deliberately about 50% accurate on hard cases; synthetic data; exploratory study.
- Crowd participants, not designers or PMs.
