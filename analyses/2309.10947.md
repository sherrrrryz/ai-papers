# How Do Analysts Understand and Verify AI-Assisted Data Analyses?

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · Verifying AI analysis (anchor for RQ1 and RQ3) · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Gu et al. built a design probe that shows an AI analysis as a natural-language explanation, code with comments, intermediate and final data tables (sortable and filterable), and per-column summary charts. 22 professionals from a large software company (program managers, engineers, consultants, finance managers; 13 of 22 only somewhat comfortable with programming or less) verified pre-generated, single-turn analyses across 10 tasks, 8 of which contained errors, in roughly 60-minute remote sessions.

## Key findings
- 52 verification workflows were observed. Procedure-oriented checking ("what did the AI do?") came first in 41 of 52; data-oriented checking ("does the result make sense?") appeared in 25 of 52.
- Participants often switched from procedure to data after spotting a possible problem (13 of 17 follow-up data checks were such switches).
- Background shaped behavior: coders read code like a code review, data-experienced analysts formed expectations before checking, and people with limited code comprehension leaned on the natural-language explanation.
- Some distrusted summary charts because they did not know how the charts were generated.
- Design recommendations: link procedure and data views (highlight the data a step produced), describe operations in plain language, state the AI's assumptions, and flag sections needing review.

## Why it relates to your idea
This is your core anchor: it shows that whether people can check an AI analysis depends on programming and analysis background, which is exactly the gap a designer or PM faces. It speaks to RQ3 (results view) and to RQ1, since your hypothesis card and event panel are a non-code "procedure view" of what the AI inferred.

## What you can take from it
- Treat the live event panel as the procedure view and the results view as the data view, and link them: clicking a funnel step on the prototype should show which event and metric produced that number.
- Prompt readers to state an expectation before seeing results (the paper recommends this sanity-check habit); a "what lift do you expect?" step in Phase 2 would do this.
- Label every chart as template-generated vs AI-generated; the paper found unclear provenance reduced trust in charts, which supports your "model writes captions only" split.
- Positioning: their sample was professionals in a data-driven company and errors were in data transformations; designers looking at experiment plans and A/B results were not studied, which is your gap.

## Limits for your use
- Tasks were data transformation sequences only, not statistical inference or experiment design.
- Participants did not write prompts or own the data, and it was single-turn; your assistant is multi-turn and acts on a repo.
