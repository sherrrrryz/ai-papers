# Facilitating Human-LLM Collaboration through Factuality Scores and Source Attributions

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ3 Reading results · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Do et al. ran a within-subjects, scenario-based online survey with 104 employees of a multinational tech company across 20 countries. Each saw a question, an LLM answer (about half accurate) and a trusted source, under a no-markup baseline, six factuality designs (color highlights on all text, highlights only below 0.5, or numeric scores, each at phrase or word level), and two source-attribution designs (reference numbers, highlighted source passages). They rated trust and ease of validation on 7-point scales and ranked preferences.

## Key findings
- All six factuality designs raised trust over baseline (2.63 to 3.21-3.62, p < .001), with no significant differences between designs.
- Ease of validation rose only for some highlight designs (e.g., word-level threshold 4.88 vs 4.29, p = .003); numeric scores did not differ from baseline.
- Highlight-everything at phrase level was preferred; 52.9% preferred phrase-level vs 26.9% word-level.
- Participants who initially rated the answer as accurate (N = 17) lowered trust after errors were flagged, while the other 87 raised it, so markup helped correct overtrust.
- Source attribution raised trust (3.40 and 3.23) but made validation harder (both 3.81 vs 4.29, p = .03). Some found markup overwhelming.

## Why it relates to your idea
Your results view pairs template charts with AI-written captions, and flags segments with too little data. This paper tests how to mark reliability on AI text for non-expert readers, which bears directly on RQ3's question of showing uncertainty without readers giving up.

## What you can take from it
- Mark uncertainty on whole phrases in the caption (e.g., color the clause "new users converted more" as low-confidence when that segment is underpowered) rather than showing a numeric score; numbers did not help validation.
- Do not assume a citation-style link from caption to chart helps checking: attribution raised trust but lowered ease of validation, a trap for your "AI-labelled caption" design.
- Measure trust and ease of validation separately in Phase 2, since they moved in different directions here.
- Give an option to hide markup, as participants found it distracting.

## Limits for your use
- Handcrafted answers and scores, one Q&A scenario, single company.
- Self-reported trust, not actual decisions; your planted trap measures behavior.
