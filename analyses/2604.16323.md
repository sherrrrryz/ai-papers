# Beyond the 'Diff': Addressing Agentic Entropy in Agentic Software Development

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ1 Planning · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
A position paper for the Human-Centered Explainable AI workshop at CHI 2026. It names "agentic entropy": autonomous coding agents optimise for local correctness while eroding global design intent. It argues that code diffs show outcomes but omit planning, tool-call sequences and cross-file decisions, and proposes "process-oriented explainability" with three pillars: conformity seeding (human-written machine-readable design rules checked before commits), reasoning monitoring (recording each tool call and reasoning step) and a causal reasoning graph with a deviation detector. There is no user study or measured result.

## Key findings
- Diffs can look harmless while violating intent; their example is a caching change that bypasses the required data-access layer yet passes tests.
- Three failure modes: local-correctness optimisation, loss of semantic stability in refactors, and a "reviewer's paradox" where output outruns human review.
- The paper claims lay "vibe coders" gain the most, because process views expose structural drift hidden behind working features; professional developers gain context without extra burden. These benefits are asserted, not tested.
- It proposes an "understanding requirement" (reviewers should be able to reconstruct the main steps) and a "cognitive debt index", not yet operationalised.

## Why it relates to your idea
Your page infers a one-line hypothesis from a code diff (RQ1). This paper argues diffs alone do not carry intent, which is a reason your hypothesis card must be editable and must invite the designer's own intent rather than present the inference as fact. It also concerns your AI assistant, which opens branches and PRs: the paper's concern about lay users overseeing agents that edit code is your scenario.

## What you can take from it
- Use it to justify the "Why" section as a human-owned field: the diff shows what changed, only the person knows why.
- "Conformity seeding" suggests a cheap move: let the designer state the intended effect first, then show where the AI's inference agrees or differs.
- Its "understanding requirement" is a usable evaluation idea: after using the page, can a participant restate what the AI changed and why? This matches your restatement measure.
- For confirmation cards, show what the agent did across files in plain words (for example "adds a click event to step 2 of checkout"), not the raw diff.

## Limits for your use
- Workshop position paper with no evaluation; good for framing only.
- Focused on architecture drift in software engineering, not experiment intent.
