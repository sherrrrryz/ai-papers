# Just-In-Time Objectives: A General Approach for Specialized AI Interactions

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ1 Planning · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Lam et al. built Poppins, which observes a screenshot or file, has a vision-language model infer the user's current objective (name, 1-2 sentence description, weight), and uses it to steer generation and LLM-as-judge evaluation. Objectives are shown to users and can be selected, edited, added or deleted. Two online studies (N = 14 with 70 inputs; N = 205 with 410 inputs) compared outputs with and without objectives, and 17 people used it in one-hour in-person sessions on their own writing.

## Key findings
- Objective-steered outputs won 70.5-85.7% of comparisons for simulated experts and 66-73% for generated tools and feedback across the two studies.
- 75.0% (Study 1) and 76.6% (Study 2) rated induced objectives accurate or very accurate.
- 97.8% of Study 2 participants chose an induced objective over writing their own.
- In person, the expert-feedback mode got higher quality ratings than a chat baseline (2.0 vs 1.41 on -3 to +3, p < .05); 82.4% vs 64.7% found it useful.
- Risks noted: users accepted objectives quickly, which can subtly steer them; latency was 1-3 minutes.

## Why it relates to your idea
Inferring a hypothesis from a code diff is a form of just-in-time objective inference. For RQ1, the paper shows inferred goals are usually judged accurate but also that people accept them almost by default, which is the exact risk your hypothesis card needs to manage.

## What you can take from it
- Copy their objective format for the hypothesis card: short name plus one or two sentences, with alternatives the user can pick between and an "add your own" option.
- Show two or three candidate hypotheses instead of one, so the user must choose; with 97.8% taking the induced option, a single suggestion invites rubber-stamping.
- Cite their steering risk as motivation for why RQ1 is about judging inferences, not just generating them.
- Measure in your study how often participants edit vs accept the inferred hypothesis, and whether accepted-unedited ones were the ones the analyst reviewer flagged.

## Limits for your use
- Writing and research tasks with people who wrote their own inputs, not product experiments.
- Win rates compare outputs, not user comprehension, which is your measure.
