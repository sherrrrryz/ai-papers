# Thinking in Graphs with CoMAP: A Shared Visual Workspace for Designing Project-Based Learning

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ2 Communicating · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Li and Jiang built CoMAP, a graph canvas for planning project-based learning with six node types, editable labelled edges, and two kinds of AI: a conversational global agent that edits the canvas and local "Refine" and "Split" agents on individual nodes that show before/after comparisons to accept or reject. In a counterbalanced within-subjects study, 30 educators (in-service and pre-service teachers, curriculum designers) used CoMAP and a dialogue-only AI plus a separate document, then shared designs with a partner.

## Key findings
- Perceived expression was higher with CoMAP (6.13 vs. 4.15, d = 1.11), as was understanding of a partner's design (5.77 vs. 4.29, d = 1.16), both p < .001.
- CoMAP scored higher on controllability (5.02 vs. 4.16), transparency (5.42 vs. 4.45), collaboration (5.72 vs. 4.49) and trust (5.55 vs. 4.57).
- Users needed fewer chat turns (11.82 vs. 18.64) and used fewer negative keywords (2.14 vs. 4.16); they accepted 76% of AI suggestions and modified 40%.
- Qualitatively, the document baseline forced "premature linearization", and the shared map gave partners something concrete to point to.
- The paper reports reduced cognitive load, but the raw ratings (5.67 vs. 4.74) make the scale direction unclear; check before citing.

## Why it relates to your idea
RQ2 asks whether an operable brief communicates better than a document with screenshots. CoMAP is a close analogue: a persistent artifact-centric workspace beat chat plus a document, and, importantly, it measured how well a *partner* understood someone else's design, which is your communication question.

## What you can take from it
- Borrow the "understanding of partner's design" measure: have one participant explain the brief to another, or have readers restate it, rather than only rating their own comprehension.
- Copy the local-agent pattern for your AI assistant: scoped actions on one element (rewrite this hypothesis, recompute this segment) with a before/after view to accept or reject, which kept users feeling in control.
- Cite it for the argument that results and plans belong on a shared artifact, not in a chat or separate document, supporting your principle "results belong on the interface."
- Note its limitation: CoMAP was tested as a whole. If you can, vary one element (e.g. live event panel on vs. off) to say what drives any effect.

## Limits for your use
- Educators designing lessons, not product teams reading experiments.
- Mostly self-reported outcomes; no feature ablation; all conditions used one model (GPT-4.1).
