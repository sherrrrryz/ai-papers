# Computer Science Achievement and Writing Skills Predict Vibe Coding Proficiency

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · Verifying AI analysis · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
A preregistered lab study at ETH Zurich with N = 100 tertiary students (51 from engineering/technology), each of whom had taken at least one intro CS course and had used LLMs for programming. Participants built three GUI web apps by prompting an LLM (Claude Sonnet 4) without seeing or editing the source code, 15 minutes per task. They also took a CS test (12-item SCS1), a reasoning test (ICAR16) and a graded essay. Blinded graders scored the apps.

## Key findings
- CS achievement correlated with vibe-coding performance at r = 0.386 (p < .001). Writing skill correlated at r = 0.290 (p = .003).
- With reasoning ability controlled, CS still predicted performance (partial r = 0.281, p = .005). Writing did not (partial r = 0.186, p = .066).
- In regression, CS alone explained R² = 0.150 and writing added ΔR² = 0.059. CS added about twice as much unique variance as writing (ΔR² = 0.125 vs 0.059). Final betas were CS 0.356 and writing 0.244.
- Mean vibe-coding score was only 0.45 of the maximum, and the decontextualized task was hardest (0.34).
- In piloting, people missed base-app features, so the platform was changed to flag untested features. Afterwards, 297 of 300 submissions had explored all features.

## Why it relates to your idea
This is direct evidence for the motivation in your proposal: even when people never touch code, CS knowledge still predicts who gets good results from AI. It sits alongside Gu et al. and *Not Everyone Wins with LLMs* as a third, larger and preregistered data point for "AI lowered the barrier to doing, not to judging." That claim is what justifies building judgment support (hypothesis card, risk cards, results view) and not just more automation.

## What you can take from it
- Cite it in your problem statement as the strongest quantitative anchor: N = 100, preregistered, and CS still matters after controlling for general reasoning.
- Use the "flag untested features" fix as a design precedent. One small nudge pushed coverage to 297 of 300. Your live event panel can do the same by marking prototype steps that have not yet been clicked or that fired no event, so "this step is not instrumented" becomes visible.
- Writing skill helped, though less than CS. That supports keeping the editable one-line hypothesis short and structured, so people who are less fluent writers are not penalized.
- The gap it leaves is that everyone sampled had CS exposure. Designers and PMs with none are exactly the group your project studies.

## Limits for your use
- Participants were students with at least one CS course, not working designers or PMs, and the task was building apps, not judging experiment output.
- The study is correlational and cross-sectional. It shows who succeeds, not which interface helps.
