# Cocoa: Co-Planning and Co-Execution with AI Agents

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ1 Planning · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Feng et al. ran a formative study with 9 PhD students, then built Cocoa, an agent inside a document editor modelled on computational notebooks. The agent (GPT-4o with Semantic Scholar tools) proposes a plan; the user edits steps, assigns each to themselves or the agent, and can run steps one at a time or continuously, switching back to planning at any point. They evaluated it in a within-subjects lab study with 16 PhD and postdoctoral researchers against a chat baseline, and a 7-day field deployment with 7 researchers.

## Key findings
- Steerability was significantly higher with Cocoa (median 4 vs. 3, p = 0.005), with no loss in ease of use (4 vs. 4.5, p = 1.000) or general utility (both 3.5).
- Users spent 15.2% of session time co-executing in Cocoa vs. 3.1% for the baseline equivalent, and 33.6% vs. 54.1% inspecting output.
- Of 86 steps, 17.4% were reassigned by users and replanning was triggered 14 times; 16.3% of surfaced output items were deleted.
- In the lab most people gave nearly all steps to the agent; in the field, delegation shifted toward users taking strategic reasoning and deep reading themselves.
- Design implications include using user steps to review risky steps before execution and setting agent control by user expertise.

## Why it relates to your idea
Your page is a shared plan between AI and a non-technical person: the AI infers hypothesis, events and metrics, the person edits and adds intent (RQ1), and the AI assistant can then act (open a branch, add instrumentation, submit a PR). Cocoa's step-level assignment and pause-for-user steps are a worked example of "AI proposes, people decide."

## What you can take from it
- Present the brief as a sequence of editable steps (hypothesis, primary metric, guardrails, duration, risks), each marked "AI drafted" or "you confirmed", so ownership is visible like Cocoa's step assignment.
- Treat each AI write (branch, PR, recompute) as a "user step" that pauses until confirmed, matching their recommendation to review risky steps before execution.
- Expect first-time users to delegate everything; in a one-session study you may need a deliberate prompt that asks them to edit or reject at least one AI-drafted element.
- Steerability vs. ease-of-use is a useful pair of Likert items for your post-task questionnaire.

## Limits for your use
- Participants were CS researchers doing literature tasks, not designers planning experiments.
- Compared only against chat, with no ablation of individual features; lab N = 16, field N = 7.
