# The Values of Value in AI Adoption: Rethinking Efficiency in UX Designers' Workplaces

*Analysis for your experiment-brief proposal · RQ2 Communicating · based on the full text*

## What the paper did
Five virtual design workshops (about 2.5 hours each, Zoom and Miro) with 15 designers, UX researchers and design consultants from finance, healthcare, education, consulting, IT/AI and retail, followed by 30-minute individual interviews. Participants worked through hypothetical adoption scenarios (top-down AI mandate, compliance-sensitive client work, team ambivalence) along a five-stage framework, and generated questions about AI use and who should raise them. Reflexive thematic analysis.

## Key findings
- Individual level: designers adopt AI mainly for efficiency, but "hidden labour" of re-prompting, checking and correcting often offsets gains; juniors worry about skill atrophy.
- Team level: colleagues submitted unverified AI outputs, including documents with fabricated or broken links; participants warned of "magic eight-ball thinking" and noted AI answers can cut short productive debate.
- AI blurs role boundaries with engineers, content designers and PMs, creating job anxiety; senior designers felt responsible for keeping juniors from over-relying on AI.
- Participants called for disclosure of AI use, comparing it to citing sources.
- Organisational level: leadership framed AI as productivity; compliance slowed approvals (one approval arrived "in week 7 of an 8-week project").

## Why it relates to your idea
Your goal is for designers to take part in experiments "with more quality" and know when to call an analyst. This paper shows designers already negotiate rigour and responsibility for AI output at team level, and that unverified AI output is a real workplace problem. It supports your RQ2 framing of the brief as a team communication object, and your principle that every AI write stops at a human-readable confirmation.

## What you can take from it
- Cite it for the motivation that designers' issue with AI is responsibility and verification, not just skill, which positions your page as rigour support rather than automation.
- Add an explicit "check with an analyst" affordance (for example on risk cards or flagged small segments), since participants want clear handoffs as role boundaries blur.
- Show who confirmed what on the brief (AI proposed, designer edited, analyst reviewed); participants equated disclosure with citing sources.
- Recruit through workplace contacts with some caution: participants described AI adoption as shaped by management pressure, which may colour how they rate an AI tool.

## Limits for your use
- Qualitative workshops on AI adoption in general, not experimentation or data.
- 15 self-selected participants with hypothetical scenarios; no measure of comprehension or behaviour.
